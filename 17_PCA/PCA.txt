1. Definition:
 PCA is a dimensionality reduction technique.
 It takes your data with many features (columns) and transforms it into a smaller set of new features (called principal components) that:
  - Still keep most of the important information.
  - Remove noise and redundancy.

2. General PCA Algorithm:
 Step 1 — Standardize the data:
    PCA is affected by scale, so we make each feature have mean = 0 and variance = 1.
           𝑋'= (𝑋−mean(𝑋))/std(𝑋)  -> std: l'ecart type

 Step 2 — Compute the covariance matrix
    The covariance matrix tells us how features vary together.
           Cov=1/(𝑚−1).transposé de 𝑋'.𝑋 -> m: number of individuals
 
 Step 3 — Find eigenvalues & eigenvectors
    Eigenvectors → directions (principal components) where the data varies the most.
    Eigenvalues → how much variance each eigenvector captures.
       ** Remarque: sum of eignvalues = number of components = inetie totale = la trace de la matrice de covariance

 Step 4 — Sort by variance
    - Sort eigenvalues from largest to smallest.
    - Keep their corresponding eigenvectors in the same order.
    - Large eigenvalue = more variance explained.

 Step 5 — Choose the number of components
    - Compute cumulative variance ratio:
         Cumulative Variance = sum of first k eigenvalues / sum of all eigenvalues
​    - Pick k so that cumulative variance ≥ your target (e.g., 80%, 90%, 95%)
